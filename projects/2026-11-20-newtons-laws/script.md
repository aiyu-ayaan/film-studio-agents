# Script: Newton's Three Laws of Motion (Class 10)

Target pace: ~2.4 words/sec (~185 words, ~75 seconds).

- **l1** (Hook): Drop a book on a desk, and it just sits there. Why? Everything that moves... or refuses to move... follows three simple rules by Isaac Newton.
- **l2** (1st Law — Balanced FBD): Newton's first law is inertia. Look at the free body diagram. Gravity pulls down with m g, and the table pushes up with normal force.
- **l3** (1st Law — Equation): Since both forces are equal and opposite, net force is zero, so acceleration is zero. An object stays at rest, or keeps moving steady, until an unbalanced force acts.
- **l4** (2nd Law — Derivation): Now push the box. Newton's second law says force equals the rate of change of momentum. Momentum p is mass times velocity, so change in momentum is m times v minus u.
- **l5** (2nd Law — Final Formula): Divide by time t, and v minus u over t is just acceleration a. That gives us the famous formula... F equals m times a, measured in Newtons.
- **l6** (2nd Law — Mass vs Acceleration): Here's the intuition. Push a light box and a heavy box with the exact same force. More mass means less acceleration.
- **l7** (3rd Law — Paired FBDs): Finally, Newton's third law. Every action has an equal and opposite reaction. Look closely at both free body diagrams.
- **l8** (3rd Law — Never Cancel): When skater A pushes skater B, force A B equals minus force B A. They never cancel out, because they act on two different bodies.
- **l9** (Recap): Zero net force means no change. F equals m a tells us how fast things speed up. And forces always come in pairs. Physics, made simple.
