Two monks were walking by a river. They came across a woman, stuck at the bank. She was afraid of the water.
The older monk didn't say a word. He lifted her, carried her across, and set her down gently.
Then the two monks walked on. For hours. The younger one stayed silent... but his mind was loud.
Finally, he couldn't hold it in. "You carried her! We're not supposed to do that!"
The older monk smiled. "I put her down at the river. Why are you still carrying her?"
That's the whole lesson. The weight was never the woman. It was the holding.
And honestly? We do this every day, in adult life. The argument from last week. The job that ended. The friend who changed. The version of you that failed.
You already put it down, out there in the real world. But in your head... you're still carrying it.
Let it go. Not for them. For your own two hands.
